The Origin

I knew what I loved
before I knew what
it was called.

When the Amul ad came on TV, I didn't watch it. I became it, every gesture, every movement, mirrored exactly. Nobody called that brand communication. I was just a kid who couldn't stay still when a mascot came on screen.

The Accident

Marketing came to me by roll number. Class 12. Summer vacation homework. Assigned, not chosen.

The students who got it did what was required. Wrote the theory, submitted the file, moved on.

I built a brand. Named it BounceNCurls. Found a white space in the Indian curly hair market. Created a logo, developed product lines, gave it an identity.

Submitted it. Went home.

But the logo stayed. The brand stayed. Not because I planned to use it, but because that project felt more real than anything I'd done academically before.

I didn't know what that feeling was called yet.

The Discovery

I was convinced the career I wanted didn't exist.

The work I was drawn to, brand identity, consumer culture, communication, felt too specific to be a real job title. So during my first year semester break, I did what I always do. I researched until I found the answer myself.

Google Digital Marketing and E-commerce specialisation. Last module. Used the tools I'd just learned to find the name for what I already was.

Brand Strategy.

Everything made sense backwards.

The Construction

I chose BBA with Marketing as my major, not by default, by direction.

Finance as my minor, deliberately. My entire family, extended included, comes from a finance background. I'm the first one going into marketing. I understood that world well enough to choose a different one, and smart enough to keep it as my foundation.

I added technology myself. Power BI, Google Analytics, HTML, Generative AI, built practically, not theoretically. Enough to read the data, use the tools, and know exactly where AI's ceiling is.

Three domains. Three different origins. All pointing the same direction.
